ML just enough for AI engineering

Why this layer matters

  • Locked docs: model cards say "validation loss", "overfits on small sets", "new embedding space". Without the vocabulary you cannot tell a real claim from marketing.
  • Locked evals: benchmark tables are loss curves, splits and contamination caveats. Misreading them costs weeks of wrong tool choices.
  • One loop: training is guess, score, adjust, repeat. Four ideas explain almost every sentence written about a model.

Conceptual model

One card per idea.

How it works

  • Loss: one number for how wrong the model is on a batch. Every training decision aims to lower it.
  • Gradient descent: the adjust step. For each weight, compute which direction lowers the loss and nudge it a tiny step that way.
  • Downhill in fog: the loss surface is the mountain, the gradient the slope under your feet. You cannot see the valley, so you follow the slope.
  • Learning rate: the step size. Too big overshoots and bounces; too small takes forever.

Trade-offs

  • pro: one picture decodes loss curves, step counts and learning-rate talk in any model card
  • con: the real surface has millions of dimensions; the mountain is a metaphor

How it works

  • Generalization: performing well on data the model never saw, the only score that matters after deployment.
  • Overfitting: memorizing training-set quirks instead of the pattern. Perfect on train, wrong on new inputs.
  • Why splits exist: you hold out a slice to catch this. Validation loss rising while training loss falls is the alarm bell.
  • Eval thinking: every benchmark claim is a generalization claim. LLMs have read much of the internet, so contamination is common and scores overstate reality. The mindset carries into evals.

Trade-offs

  • pro: one alarm bell, train and validation losses diverging, catches memorization early
  • con: clean splits are hard to guarantee once training data is web-scale

How it works

  • Meaning as geometry: an maps text or an image to one point in a space of hundreds or thousands of dimensions.
  • Distance is relatedness: nearby points mean similar things: "invoice total" sits near "amount due", far from "golden retriever". Cosine similarity is the usual ruler.
  • Direction is relation: the offset from "France" to "Paris" roughly equals "Japan" to "Tokyo". Relationships live in directions, not just positions.
  • Why you care: the machinery under semantic search, clustering and RAG, covered fully in embeddings.

Trade-offs

  • pro: similarity becomes math you can index, cluster and search at scale
  • con: geometry is only as good as its training; lookalike nonsense can sit near the truth

How it works

  • Scaling laws: with more parameters, data and compute, loss falls smoothly and predictably for a long run.
  • Diminishing returns: each doubling of compute buys a smaller step down; power-law curves quietly flatten in payoff.
  • Practical read: frontier capability is bought with pretraining-scale compute. A 500-example fine-tune mostly shifts style and format, not reliable world knowledge.
  • Data quality beats volume: past a point, cleaner, better-labeled data beats more of it.

Trade-offs

  • pro: tells you what a fine-tune can and cannot buy before you pay for one
  • con: the flattening is quiet; doubling spend late in the curve buys little

Decision boundary

Use when

  • A model card or benchmark table is the input to a real model or tool decision
  • A vendor benchmark needs interrogation: what was held out, and did it leak into training
  • Someone proposes a fine-tune and you must judge what it can shift, see fine-tuning vs RAG vs prompting

Skip when

  • The work is prompt and API plumbing on a fixed, already-chosen model
  • A throwaway demo is the goal and nobody is citing eval numbers
  • The model is fixed by platform or policy and no benchmark claim will move the choice

Worked example

The whole training loop in four lines: loss is the score, gradient the slope, learning rate the step. You will see this shape in countless tools and logs.

# guess, score, adjust, repeat
for step in range(steps):
    loss = score(model(batch), targets)    # how wrong, one number
    grads = gradient(loss, model.weights)  # slope under each weight
    model.weights -= lr * grads            # small step downhill

Multimodal, vision, OCR

Why this layer matters

  • Half the data is not text: invoices, receipts, screenshots, scans, table-heavy contracts, voice notes. A text-only pipeline ignores all of it.
  • Naive fix fails: classic OCR on everything loses layout, handwriting and charts, often exactly the information you needed.
  • Native multimodality: frontier LLMs read images natively, so a separate vision microservice is often unnecessary plumbing in 2026 stacks.

Conceptual model

Three readers for pixels: pick per input type, not per fashion.

How it works

  • Detects glyphs, returns characters plus bounding boxes. Deterministic, cheap, scales to millions of pages.
  • Tools: Tesseract for open source, or managed AWS Textract, Google Document AI, Azure Read.
  • Preprocessing decides quality: deskew and denoise first, or confidence tanks.
  • Per-word confidence gives an audit trail and a fallback trigger.

Trade-offs

  • pro: deterministic, auditable, cheapest per page, exact on IDs and amounts
  • con: loses meaning, tables, handwriting, and intent

How it works

  • Image plus text prompt in, text out: the usual chat API with one extra image content part.
  • Handles messy layouts, charts, rotation, handwriting, crossed-out form fields, screenshots.
  • Resolution: large images get downscaled or into crops. Small text can land between tiles or vanish in downscaling.
  • Billing: image tokens, often hundreds to a few thousand per image depending on resolution.

Trade-offs

  • pro: judgment, layout understanding and contextual extraction in one call
  • con: nondeterministic, can invent text, cost scales with resolution

How it works

  • Split roles: OCR returns line items and totals exactly, the VLM recovers table structure, a cheap text-only LLM maps the merged text onto your schema.
  • Join by coordinates: place OCR words into the cells the VLM located; never let the VLM transcribe critical numbers.
  • Keep a VLM fallback path for pages where OCR confidence drops.

Trade-offs

  • pro: exactness plus structure plus an audit trail of source boxes
  • con: two systems to maintain, coordinate joining is fiddly

Audio rides the same rail: STT (Whisper or managed APIs) turns speech into text for a normal text pipeline, and TTS voices the answer. Voice earns its keep when hands are busy, speaking beats typing, or accessibility requires it; otherwise text is cheaper, faster and easier to log.

Decision boundary

Use when

  • OCR: millions of pages, clean or born-digital scans, exact characters matter (IDs, amounts), tight unit cost
  • VLM: messy layouts, charts, screenshots, handwriting, low page counts, judgment or extraction with context
  • Hybrid: production document extraction needing exactness and structure, plus an audit trail of source boxes

Skip when

  • OCR: handwriting, tables whose structure you need, any call requiring meaning or judgment
  • VLM: very high volume on a tight budget, or exactness with no verification step
  • Hybrid: a prototype where one VLM call is accurate enough, or sources that are already text

Worked example

Two real shapes: one VLM call for receipts and screenshots, and the hybrid pattern when exactness and structure both matter.

  • Screenshot to code: pure VLM territory; the information is pixels and layout that no text-first stage can see.
  • Document QA with figures: RAG over OCR text answers most questions; figure questions route to a VLM with the page image attached. Routing is introduced in RAG big picture.
from openai import OpenAI

client = OpenAI()

# image + instruction in, structured JSON out, one call
resp = client.chat.completions.create(
    model="gpt-4o-mini",
    messages=[{
        "role": "user",
        "content": [
            {"type": "text",
             "text": "Extract vendor, date, total. JSON only."},
            {"type": "image_url",
             "image_url": {"url": "data:image/png;base64," + img_b64}},
        ],
    }],
    response_format={"type": "json_object"},
)
print(resp.choices[0].message.content)

# hybrid pattern: OCR owns the words, VLM owns the structure
words = ocr(image)           # Tesseract or managed OCR, text + boxes
layout = vlm(image, "Return table cell boxes as JSON. Do not read the text.")
cells = snap(words, layout)  # place words into cells by coordinates
facts = llm_to_schema(cells) # cheap text-only extraction from here on

if ocr_confidence(words) < 0.9:
    facts = vlm(image, schema) # fallback: let the VLM read directly

Failure modes

  • Image token cost: often hundreds to a few thousand tokens per image. A vision-heavy agent loop multiplies this fast; budget it like any spend in tokens and cost.
  • Resolution and tiling: small text can fall between tiles or vanish in downscaling, and tiles can cut a table apart. Test your worst scan, not your best.
  • Hallucinated text: a VLM can read characters that are not there, filling in plausible amounts and IDs. Verify critical fields against OCR boxes, checksums or totals math.
  • PII in images: faces, signatures, license plates and account numbers ship straight to a vendor API. Redact first, or self-host the reader for sensitive flows.
  • Injected instructions: text inside an image is model input; a scanned form reading "ignore previous instructions" is a real attack surface, see prompt injection.
  • Silent degradation: OCR quality drifts with scan quality and upstream changes. Track confidence distributions over time, not one golden test.

Keep in mind

  • Loss is wrongness, gradient descent is the downhill walk, learning rate is the step size.
  • Generalization is the only real score. Validation splits exist to catch memorization.
  • Embeddings turn meaning into geometry: distance is relatedness, direction is relation.
  • Scaling smooths out then flattens. A 500-example fine-tune mostly changes style, not knowledge.
  • Exact text at scale: OCR. Meaning and layout: VLM. Production extraction: hybrid, joined by geometry.
  • Images are billed as tokens and can hallucinate. Verify amounts, redact PII, expect injection in pixels.
