You will not train models in this job. You still need the handful of words that make model cards and eval tables readable, plus a plan for the half of real-world data that arrives as images and audio instead of text.

ML just enough for AI engineering

Why you need it
  • Locked docs: model cards say "validation loss", "overfits on small sets", "new embedding space". Without the vocabulary you cannot tell a real claim from a marketing one.
  • Locked evals: benchmark tables are loss curves, splits, and contamination caveats. Misreading them costs weeks of wrong tool choices.
  • One loop: training is guess, score, adjust, repeat. Four core ideas explain almost every sentence written about a model.
Solutions

The vocabulary itself, one expandable card per idea.

How it works

  • Loss: one number that says how wrong the model is on a batch of examples. Lower is better, every training decision aims at this number.
  • Gradient descent: the adjust step. For each internal weight, compute which direction would lower the loss, then nudge it a tiny step that way.
  • Downhill in fog: the loss surface is the mountain, the gradient is the slope under your feet. You cannot see the valley, so you follow the slope step by step.
  • Learning rate: the step size. Too big and you overshoot and bounce around, too small and training takes forever.

Trade-offs

  • pro: one picture decodes loss curves, step counts, and learning-rate talk in any model card
  • con: the real surface has millions of dimensions, the mountain is only a metaphor

How it works

  • Generalization: performing well on data the model never saw. The only score that matters after deployment.
  • Overfitting: memorizing quirks of the training set instead of learning the pattern. Perfect on train, wrong on new inputs.
  • Why splits exist: you hold out a slice precisely to catch this. Validation loss rising while training loss falls is the alarm bell.
  • Eval thinking: every benchmark claim is a generalization claim. LLMs have read much of the internet, so contamination is common and scores overstate reality. This mindset carries into evals.

Trade-offs

  • pro: one alarm bell, train and validation losses diverging, catches memorization early
  • con: clean splits are hard to guarantee once training data is web-scale

How it works

  • Meaning as geometry: an maps text (or an image) to a long list of numbers, one point in a space of hundreds or thousands of dimensions.
  • Distance is relatedness: nearby points mean similar things. "invoice total" sits near "amount due" and far from "golden retriever". Cosine similarity is the usual ruler.
  • Direction is relation: the offset from "France" to "Paris" roughly equals the offset from "Japan" to "Tokyo". Relationships live in directions, not just positions.
  • Why you care: this is the machinery under semantic search, clustering, and RAG. It gets the full treatment in embeddings.

Trade-offs

  • pro: similarity becomes math you can index, cluster, and search at scale
  • con: geometry is only as good as its training, lookalike nonsense can sit near the truth

How it works

  • Scaling laws: more parameters, more data, more compute, and loss falls smoothly and surprisingly predictably for a long run.
  • Diminishing returns: each doubling of compute buys a smaller step down. Power-law curves look endless and quietly flatten in payoff.
  • Practical read: frontier capability is bought with pretraining-scale compute. A fine-tune on 500 examples shifts style and format, not world knowledge.
  • Data quality beats volume: past a point, cleaner and better-labeled data outperforms simply adding more of it.

Trade-offs

  • pro: tells you what a fine-tune can and cannot buy before you pay for one
  • con: the flattening is quiet, doubling spend late in the curve buys little
When to use · when not

Use when

  • A model card or benchmark table is the input to a real model or tool decision
  • A vendor benchmark needs interrogation: what was held out, and did it leak into training
  • Someone proposes a fine-tune and you must judge what it can actually shift, that call gets its own page in fine-tuning vs RAG vs prompting

Skip when

  • The work is prompt and API plumbing on a fixed, already-chosen model
  • A throwaway demo is the goal and nobody is citing eval numbers
  • The model is fixed by platform or policy and no benchmark claim will move the choice
Example

The whole training loop in four lines. Loss is the score, the gradient is the slope, the learning rate is the step. You will read this shape a hundred times in other people's tooling and logs.

# guess, score, adjust, repeat
for step in range(steps):
    loss = score(model(batch), targets)    # how wrong, one number
    grads = gradient(loss, model.weights)  # slope under each weight
    model.weights -= lr * grads            # small step downhill

Multimodal, vision, OCR

Why you need it
  • Half the data is not text: invoices, receipts, screenshots, scans, contracts full of tables, voice notes. A text-only pipeline pretends none of this exists.
  • Naive fix fails: bolt classic OCR onto everything and you lose layout, handwriting, charts, and confidence signals, often exactly the information you needed.
  • Native multimodality: frontier LLMs see images out of the box, so a separate vision microservice is often unnecessary plumbing in 2026 stacks.
Solutions

Three readers for pixels, pick per input type, not per fashion.

How it works

  • Detects glyphs, returns characters plus bounding boxes. Deterministic, cheap, scales to millions of pages.
  • Tools: Tesseract for open source, or managed AWS Textract, Google Document AI, Azure Read.
  • Preprocessing quality decides everything: deskew and denoise first, or confidence tanks.
  • Per-word confidence scores give you an audit trail and a trigger for fallback.

Trade-offs

  • pro: deterministic, auditable, cheapest per page, exact on IDs and amounts
  • con: loses meaning, tables, handwriting, and intent

How it works

  • Takes an image plus a text prompt, returns text. Same chat API shape as always, one extra content part carrying the image.
  • Handles messy layouts, charts, rotation, handwriting, crossed-out form fields, screenshots.
  • Large images get downscaled or into crops. Small text can land between tiles or vanish in downscaling.
  • Billed in image tokens, often hundreds to a few thousand per image depending on resolution.

Trade-offs

  • pro: judgment, layout understanding, and extraction with context in a single call
  • con: nondeterministic, can invent text, cost scales with resolution

How it works

  • OCR returns line items and totals exactly, the VLM recovers table structure, a cheap text-only LLM maps the merged text onto your schema.
  • Join by coordinates: place words into the cells the VLM located, never let the VLM transcribe critical numbers.
  • Keep a VLM fallback path for pages where OCR confidence drops.

Trade-offs

  • pro: exactness plus structure plus an audit trail of source boxes
  • con: two systems to maintain, coordinate joining is fiddly

Audio rides the same rail: STT (Whisper or managed APIs) turns speech into text, and from there it is a normal text pipeline. TTS renders answers back into voice. Voice loops earn their keep when hands are busy, speaking is faster than typing, or accessibility requires it. Otherwise text in and text out is cheaper, faster, and easier to log.

When to use · when not

Use when

  • OCR: millions of pages, clean or born-digital scans, exact characters matter (IDs, amounts), tight unit cost
  • VLM: messy layouts, charts, screenshots, handwriting, low page counts, judgment or extraction with context
  • Hybrid: production document extraction needing exactness and structure, plus an audit trail of source boxes

Skip when

  • OCR: handwriting, tables whose structure you need, any call requiring meaning or judgment
  • VLM: very high volume on a tight budget, or exactness with no verification step
  • Hybrid: a prototype where one VLM call is accurate enough, or sources that are already text
Example

Two real shapes. A single VLM call for receipts and screenshots, and the hybrid pattern for document pipelines where exactness and structure both matter.

  • Screenshot to code: pure VLM territory, the information is pixels and layout, no text-first stage can even see it.
  • Document QA with figures: RAG over OCR text answers most questions, while figure questions route to a VLM with the page image attached. Routing is introduced in RAG big picture.
from openai import OpenAI

client = OpenAI()

# image + instruction in, structured JSON out, one call
resp = client.chat.completions.create(
    model="gpt-4o-mini",
    messages=[{
        "role": "user",
        "content": [
            {"type": "text",
             "text": "Extract vendor, date, total. JSON only."},
            {"type": "image_url",
             "image_url": {"url": "data:image/png;base64," + img_b64}},
        ],
    }],
    response_format={"type": "json_object"},
)
print(resp.choices[0].message.content)

# hybrid pattern: OCR owns the words, VLM owns the structure
words = ocr(image)           # Tesseract or managed OCR, text + boxes
layout = vlm(image, "Return table cell boxes as JSON. Do not read the text.")
cells = snap(words, layout)  # place words into cells by coordinates
facts = llm_to_schema(cells) # cheap text-only extraction from here on

if ocr_confidence(words) < 0.9:
    facts = vlm(image, schema) # fallback: let the VLM read directly
Watch out
  • Image token cost: providers bill images as tokens, often hundreds to a few thousand per image. A vision-heavy agent loop multiplies this fast, budget it like any spend in tokens and cost.
  • Resolution and tiling: small text can land between tiles or vanish in downscaling, and tiling can cut a table across crops. Test with your worst scan, not your best.
  • Hallucinated text: a VLM can read characters that are not there, filling in plausible amounts and IDs. Verify critical fields against OCR boxes, checksums, or totals math.
  • PII in images: faces, signatures, license plates, and account numbers ship straight to a vendor API. Redact before sending, or self-host the reader for sensitive flows.
  • Injected instructions: text inside an image is model input. A scanned form printed with "ignore previous instructions" is a real attack surface, covered in prompt injection.
  • Silent degradation: OCR quality drifts with scan quality and upstream changes. Track confidence distributions over time instead of trusting one golden test.
Remember
  • Loss is wrongness, gradient descent is the downhill walk, learning rate is the step size.
  • Generalization is the only real score. Validation splits exist to catch memorization.
  • Embeddings turn meaning into geometry: distance is relatedness, direction is relation.
  • Scaling smooths out then flattens. A 500-example fine-tune changes style, not knowledge.
  • Exact text at scale: OCR. Meaning and layout: VLM. Production extraction: hybrid, joined by geometry.
  • Images are billed as tokens and can hallucinate. Verify amounts, redact PII, expect injection in pixels.
