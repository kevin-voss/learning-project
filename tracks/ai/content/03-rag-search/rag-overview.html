RAG the big picture

A base model only knows its training data. Retrieval puts the right few paragraphs of fresh, private text in front of the model per question, then makes it answer and cite what it was shown. No retraining, ever.

Why you need it
  • Frozen knowledge: the model has never seen your wiki, contracts or tickets. Its knowledge stopped at the training cutoff.
  • Confident invention: ask anyway and it fills the gaps with fluent, plausible, unverifiable text. A brilliant consultant with amnesia.
  • Pasting everything fails: context is finite and metered (see context budget). 500 pages × 10k queries a day is a real invoice, and recall still droops mid-window.
  • Fine-tuning the facts in fails: weights teach style, not facts (see the fine-tune decision). Nothing trained today knows tomorrow's prices.
  • The real need: the right few paragraphs of fresh, private, citable text per question, without retraining anything.
Solutions

Four ways to ground an answer in your facts. Retrieval wins most of them, the alternatives win niches.

How it works

  • = fetch, then answer. An open-book exam, set fresh at every question. Look up passages from YOUR data, put them in the prompt, answer grounded in them, cite them.
  • Two pipelines, keep them separate in your head and your code: an offline ingest batch job and an online query hot path.
  • You never retrain. Freshness is a file drop. Permissions live in the retrieval layer, filtering before the model sees a token.
  • What you buy: citations for free, private data stays home, flat cost per question regardless of corpus size (see token cost).

Ingestion and parsing · where RAG pipelines quietly die

  • A PDF is a printout, not a document: characters at coordinates. Two-column layouts, tables as positioned text, scanned pages that are just photographs.
  • Native text vs OCR: born-digital PDFs, HTML and Office files carry an exact text layer, extraction is free and faithful. Scans are pictures, OCR has to guess the letters, and every guessed error flows downstream into embeddings. More on OCR and vision models in ML and multimodal.
  • The parser ladder: escalate spend only as the document resists. Layout parsers (Docling, Unstructured, LlamaParse) for born-digital files, classic OCR (Tesseract, PaddleOCR) for clean scans, cloud document AI (Azure DI, Google DocAI, Textract) for forms and tables at scale, a vision LLM for horror layouts, ColPali-style page-image retrieval when text is hopeless.
  • why markdown headings become chunk boundaries for chunking, code fences stay atomic, lists stay lists. Structure is exactly what the chunker cuts on.
  • Tables are the boss fight: keep each table atomic (one table = one chunk) or serialize rows to markdown or JSON. A table shredded across three chunks answers nothing.
  • Incremental sync: watch the source, re-parse only changed documents, delete-and-replace by document id, dedupe on the way in. The same PDF uploaded five times becomes five near-identical chunk sets that crowd out diversity.
  • Source of truth: the index is disposable, always rebuildable from the source system. Permissions, deletes and versions flow from the source, never from the index's own state.
  • gotcha failures are invisible: the pipeline never errors. A merged table column or a glued header surfaces as wrong answers months later, not as parse logs. Parsing is the number one silent RAG killer.
  • cost parsing is your biggest ingest line item. It touches every page exactly once per run, and per-page prices compound across every re-ingestion. Get the ladder right before scaling.

Naive RAG failure modes

  • Retrieval miss: the model cannot use a passage you never fetched. Most "the LLM is wrong" incidents are "retrieval missed the passage" incidents. Fixes live in hybrid search and rerank.
  • Vocabulary gap: users say "time off", the docs say "PTO accrual". Pure vector search can miss, keyword fusion rescues.
  • Top-k redundancy: five near-identical chunks fill the window with one idea and crowd out coverage of the rest.
  • Ignored context: the model answers from its weights despite the passages in front of it. Enforce "answer only from these passages" and require a citation per claim.
  • No honest miss: without an abstention path, "not in my docs" becomes a hallucination instead of an answer.
  • Stale index: documents changed upstream, the index never heard. Wire sync to source events or answers quietly rot.

Trade-offs

  • pro: fresh, private, citable answers at flat cost per question, no retraining ever.
  • pro: permissions and freshness handled in the retrieval layer, before the model sees a token.
  • con: it is a system, not a feature: parsing, chunking, search and evals, and one weak link caps everything downstream.
  • con: retrieval quality is the ceiling. Generate-side upgrades cannot recover an unfetched passage.

How it works

  • Skip retrieval entirely: paste a few hundred static pages straight into a long-context window and ask.
  • Legitimately fine for a small, static, rarely queried doc set. A demo, an internal FAQ, one contract.

Trade-offs

  • pro: zero infrastructure, no pipeline to build, debug or keep fresh.
  • con: cost scales per query, you pay for the whole corpus on every single question (see token cost).
  • con: recall droops mid-window on long prompts (see context budget).
  • con: citations never arrive, the model just read a wall of text.
  • con: live, large or private corpora keep needing retrieval anyway.

How it works

  • Train the model on examples so behavior sticks: style, tone, output format, domain vocabulary.
  • It does not stick for facts: weights teach style, not knowledge. Nothing trained today knows tomorrow's prices. Full decision in fine-tune vs RAG.

Trade-offs

  • pro: cheap at inference, format and tone follow the training data.
  • con: no citations, frozen knowledge, retrain per base-model upgrade.
  • con: teaching fresh or changing facts this way is a dead end, pair it with retrieval instead.

How it works

  • No retrieval, no fine-tune: ask and trust the training data. Fine for demos and throwaway projects over small static fact sets.

Trade-offs

  • pro: zero setup, the fastest path to a first prototype.
  • con: no citations, no privacy story, staleness is silent, anything private is out of reach.

Each pipeline stage has its own deep dive: parsing above, then chunking, embeddings and search and rerank.

When to use · when not

Use when

  • The corpus is large, private or live, and citations are part of the product.
  • Facts change faster than any training cycle could follow.
  • Cost per question should stay flat as the corpus grows.
  • Permissions must filter answers per user or tenant.

Skip when

  • The doc set is tiny and static, pasting it whole is simpler.
  • You only want style, tone or format changes: prompt or fine-tune instead (see fine-tune vs RAG).
  • It is a demo or throwaway, grounding does not matter yet.
  • You cannot staff the pipeline: parsing, chunking, search and evals all need owners.

Watch out
  • Silent parse failure: no error, just confidently wrong answers about the one table the user cared about · inspect parsed output for your worst pages before scaling (see evals).
  • Per-page cost compounds: parsing touches every page once per re-ingestion run · vision-LLM parsing at scale is a budget decision, not an afterthought.
  • Vision parsing invents details: validate critical fields against source systems, keep page images for audit.
  • Duplicate ingestion: the same file five times means five overlapping chunk sets crowding out diversity · hash or content-match on the way in.
  • Retrieval is the ceiling: debug "wrong answer" as a retrieval bug first, a generation bug second.
  • Stale index: answers keep quoting deleted policy · tie deletions and updates to source-of-truth events.
Remember
  • Two code paths, one index: offline ingest batch job, online query hot path.
  • Parse to structure-aware markdown before any chunking, tables and headings intact.
  • Escalate parser spend only as documents resist: native text, then layout, then OCR, then vision.
  • Dedupe and delete-and-replace on every sync. The index is disposable, the source system is not.
  • Filter permissions in retrieval, before the model sees a token.
  • Prompt for citations and honest misses. Evaluate retrieval separately from generation.
