Search and reranking

Every corpus gets asked two kinds of questions: "cozy winter jacket" and "XR-2500-B". The first needs meaning, the second needs exact characters, and no single search lane serves both. Production retrieval runs both lanes, merges them by rank, then spends a slow careful expert on the finalists only.

Why you need it
  • Meaning queries: "cozy winter jacket". Exact words fail, paraphrase power wins. Dense vectors from embeddings own this half.
  • Identifier queries: "XR-2500-B", "ERR_CONN_RESET_7", "section 4.2". Vectors are hopeless, no embedding knows which SKU matters, and paraphrase power actively hurts.
  • Single-lane systems fail the half they were not built for, and users cannot tell you in advance which half they are in.
Solutions

Two cheap lanes, one merge, one slow expert. Search quality in one line: right lane, good merge, honest second opinion.

How it works

  • Smart word counting: . weights rare terms heavily, rewards but saturates repeats, length normalization keeps big documents from winning just by being big.
  • Saturation (k1 ~ 1.2): a document mentioning "refund" ten times is not ten times more relevant. Also kills keyword spam.
  • Length normalization (b ~ 0.75): long documents mention everything by accident. Without b they out-score tight answers for sheer size.
  • Why it still wins on exact terms: identifiers, IDs, error codes, function names and rare tokens are characters, not meaning. No vector model knows your SKU catalog. BM25 matches them verbatim, ranks deterministically, and explains itself.
  • Cost profile: an inverted index, no model calls, sub-millisecond queries. Postgres ships it as tsvector + ts_rank_cd.
  • Known weakness: typos and morphology. Add fuzziness or stemming where users type.

Trade-offs

  • pro: sub-millisecond, no model calls, deterministic and explainable
  • con: paraphrase, synonyms and cross-language all miss, typos wreck it without fuzziness

How it works

  • Strength: synonyms, paraphrase, cross-language, "find similar". "Money-back guarantee" retrieves the refund policy, see embeddings.
  • Honest blind spots: exact identifiers, negation ("no refund" sits NEAR "refund"), rare entities, out-of-vocabulary codes.
  • Learned sparse (): a model expands each document with implied terms. Best of both lanes, bigger indexes, less explainability.

Trade-offs

  • pro: owns the meaning half of traffic, no keyword overlap required
  • con: blind on identifiers, negation and rare codes, quality capped by the embedding model

How it works

  • Run BM25 AND dense retrieval on every query, then merge the two ranked lists with .
  • why fuse ranks not scores BM25 scores are unbounded sums, cosine lives in [-1, 1]. Averaging them is unit soup. Ranks are universal: score = sum of 1/(60 + rank), where k ~ 60 is folklore that damps top-rank dominance just right.
  • Why hybrid is the modern default: users type both "the Q3 refund thing" and "XR-2500-B", the query mix is unknowable in advance, and one weak lane costs you the half of traffic it cannot serve. Two cheap indexes beat one clever one.

Trade-offs

  • pro: serves both halves of traffic, no lane guessing per query
  • con: two indexes to operate, k = 60 is a start not law

How it works

  • A reads query AND document together through one model, so it sees token-level interactions a bi-encoder (two separate vectors) never can. Far more accurate, and you pay per pair.
  • Stage 2, not stage 1: retrieve top-50 or top-100 cheap, rerank narrow to top-5 or top-10. Scoring the whole corpus with a cross-encoder is hopelessly slow.
  • best cheap win the biggest quality-per-effort jump in retrieval. Same index, same first stage, one extra model call over 50 candidates.
  • Budget: adds roughly 100 to 300 ms. Batch the candidates, cache popular queries, see caching.

The pipeline · recall wide, rank narrow

  • Recall wide: hybrid retrieval pulls the top-100 candidates. Quantity over precision, cheap and fast.
  • Rerank narrow: the cross-encoder reorders those candidates and keeps the top-5.
  • Stuff context: the survivors go into the prompt, ordered best-first, with source citations attached. Sizing rules live in context budgeting.
  • Query surgery before any search: rewrite typos and abbreviations, decompose multi-part questions, generate 3 query variants and union the hits, try to close the question-vs-document gap. Each transform is another LLM call, so measure whether it earns its latency.

Trade-offs

  • pro: top-5 precision soars, one extra call on an existing index
  • con: 100 to 300 ms added, per-pair cost, useless over wide candidate sets

measuring retrieval

  • : did the right chunk make it into the candidates at all? Tuning target for the hybrid stage.
  • : where does the first good hit land? Tuning target for the rerank stage.
  • Golden questions: 30 to 100 real user questions with known good answers and labeled relevant chunks. Run them on every retrieval change, catch regressions before users do. Wire this into evals.
When to use · when not

Use when

  • Exact identifiers dominate → BM25 lane
  • Natural-language and find-similar → dense lane
  • Query mix unknowable → hybrid + RRF, the default
  • Top-5 precision matters → rerank the top-50
  • Vague, multi-part or mismatched queries → query transforms

Skip when

  • Skip BM25-only for paraphrase or cross-language traffic
  • Skip dense-only on identifier-heavy corpora
  • Skip cross-encoder scoring of the full corpus, hopelessly slow
  • Skip query transforms on simple FAQ traffic, just latency

Example

RRF in nine lines of python, then the same hybrid pipeline in one Postgres round trip: both lanes, ranks fused by RRF, top-5 out.

def rrf(ranked_lists, k=60, top_k=5):
    """Merge ranked lists without score-scale headaches."""
    scores = {}
    for ranked in ranked_lists:               # BM25 hits + dense hits
        for rank, doc_id in enumerate(ranked):   # rank 0 = best
            scores[doc_id] = scores.get(doc_id, 0) + 1.0 / (k + rank + 1)
    return sorted(scores, key=scores.get, reverse=True)[:top_k]
-- pgvector hybrid: BM25 lane + ANN lane, fused by RRF, one round trip
WITH keyword AS (
  SELECT id, row_number() OVER (ORDER BY ts_rank_cd(tsv, q) DESC) AS rank
  FROM chunks, websearch_to_tsquery('english', $1) q
  WHERE tsv @@ q                      $3 = tenant_id goes here too
  LIMIT 100
),
semantic AS (
  SELECT id, row_number() OVER (ORDER BY embedding <=> $2) AS rank
  FROM chunks
  WHERE tenant_id = $3                  $2 = normalized query embedding
  LIMIT 100
)
SELECT id
FROM (
  SELECT id, rank FROM keyword
  UNION ALL
  SELECT id, rank FROM semantic
) lanes
GROUP BY id
ORDER BY sum(1.0 / (60 + rank)) DESC   -- RRF, k = 60
LIMIT 5;

e-commerce · both lanes

  • BM25 for SKUs and model numbers, dense for "cozy office chair".
  • Rerank the top-50 so the first product row is the right one.
  • One hybrid index pair, no lane guessing per query.

enterprise wiki · unknowable mix

  • Users really do type both "the Q3 refund thing" and "XR-2500-B".
  • Hybrid underneath, golden questions guarding every change.
  • Tenant filter applied inside both lanes, never after.
Watch out
  • Filter-before vs filter-after: pre-filtering can starve the ANN index (fewer candidates than k, or a filter the index cannot push down), post-filtering can shrink a top-100 down to three usable hits. Behavior differs per store, test with your narrowest real filters, prefer indexes that filter during traversal.
  • Tenant isolation: one missing tenant_id predicate and another tenant's chunks land in the prompt, a data leak straight into generation. Filter in both lanes, enforce it at the query builder layer, test with a cross-tenant canary query.
  • Semantic cache lies: a nearest-neighbor cache hit answers a similar, not identical, question. Scope entries per tenant and per corpus version, set a similarity floor, invalidate on re-index, see caching.
  • Reranking too wide: a cross-encoder over 500 candidates buys little accuracy and burns 2 to 3 seconds. Keep stage 1 wide, stage 2 at top-50.
  • Unmeasured tuning: tweaking k, ef_search or rerank thresholds without recall@k and MRR on golden questions is vibes. Regressions ship silently.
  • Score soup: merging BM25 and cosine scores by weighted sum instead of ranks breaks the moment either model changes scale. Ranks are the stable currency.
Remember
  • Default to hybrid: BM25 + dense on every query, fused with RRF at k ~ 60.
  • Rerank the top-50 with a cross-encoder, feed the top-5 into the context budget.
  • Keep exact-match traffic on the keyword lane, it is faster and explainable.
  • Apply tenant and permission filters inside every lane, test with canary queries.
  • Guard every retrieval change with recall@k, MRR and golden questions in evals.
  • Cache popular queries only with a similarity floor and per-tenant scope.
